# CAIRN — Data Sources

Every map layer, photo and reading in CAIRN, per site. Generated by `data-pipeline/build_docs.py` from the same
provenance records that feed the *Data sources* panel in the app.

## Jezero Crater — Neretva Vallis / Bright Angel

Rover: **Perseverance** (Mars 2020). EVA date: sol 1215 (Sol Perseverance cored Cheyava Falls (Sapphire Canyon)).

- **Walk area:** 18.4754° to 18.5100° N, 77.2889° to 77.3234° E (2.048 km square), 1025 × 1025 grid at 2 m
- **Relief:** 423 m; median slope 9.5°; 3.2% steeper than 25°
- **Projection:** Equirectangular, Mars 2000 sphere (R = 3,396,190 m), lat_ts = 0, lon0 = 0.0

**Notes**

- *Surface image (ground texture):* HiRISE is a single-band (grey) image here; the Mars colour in the 3D view is a display tint, not measured colour.
- *Night-time thermal infrared:* Native resolution is ~100 m, so this layer is smooth at walking scale.
- *Geologic units:* Mapped at 1:75,000, so unit boundaries are approximate at walking scale.
- *Minerals (olivine, Fe/Mg clay, carbonate):* CRISM imaged the north-east quarter of the walk area. Strength is scaled to these observations (Viviano-Beck et al. 2014 parameters).
- *Loose sand hazard:* This is our own analysis, not an official NASA product.
- *Terrain shielding (sky view):* Model, not a measurement: the zenith exponent is an assumption.
- *Surface radiation dose rate:* Curiosity is in Gale crater, about 3,700 km from Jezero. The archive holds 2–4 hours of dosimetry per sol, so the first ~13 hours of the 20 May 2024 storm are not in it. That gap is filled by a clearly labelled model scaled to NASA's reported total of 8,100 micrograys.

## The 20 May 2024 storm replay (both sites)

- **Eruption:** DONKI `2024-05-20T05:36:00-CME-001` — R-type CME, 2678 km/s. <https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/DONKI/view/CME/31034/-1>
- **Notification:** `20240520-AL-001`, 10.3 h after the eruption. <https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/DONKI/view/Alert/31039/1>
- **Measured:** 85 Curiosity RAD dose-rate observations, 18–24 May 2024 (MSL RAD RDR, PDS PPI node (MSL-M-RAD-3-RDR-V1.0), detector E dose rate). Background 5.39 µGy/h.
- **Reported by NASA:** NASA/JPL, 10 June 2024: an astronaut standing next to Curiosity would have received 8,100 micrograys, comparable to 30 chest X-rays.
- **Model (not a measurement):** Fast spike (linear rise, exponential decay) plus slow tail. The tail is fitted to the RAD readings 13-43 h after onset; the spike is scaled so the event total above background equals NASA's reported 8,100 micrograys. Peak 3100 µGy/h.

## Reproducing the data

```bash
cd data-pipeline
make setup && make fetch && make build   # Jezero
make gale                                 # Gale (after downloading its HiRISE files)
python build_docs.py
```
